# Band-pass sensitivity and signal-validity audit

Generated by `scripts/evaluate_bandpass.py`. Retrospective analysis added on 2026-10-07 after review; not preregistered.

## Sampling rate

The authors do not state the EMG sampling rate for this export. Every one of the 88 exported clocks gives (samples − 1) / elapsed time between 1923.91 and 1925.93 Hz (median 1925.92 Hz). The agreement supports using the median as an average-rate estimate for sensitivity analysis. Nondecreasing clocks are required, while duplicates are retained. Uniform acquisition is an explicit assumption: endpoint agreement cannot establish the cause of duplicated times or rule out missing samples. Rounding is a possible explanation, not a demonstrated cause. Original device preprocessing remains undocumented.

## Band-pass specification

A Butterworth prototype N=4 band-pass (order 8 per direction, effective order 16 after forward/backward filtering) of 20–450 Hz (a common surface-EMG band) was applied to each record and all 21 amplitude specifications were recomputed (1848 evaluations). It is an additional specification, not a replacement for the exported baseline.

Across the 43 primary pairs, whole-record RMS direction changes after band-pass: 1 — patient7/iso180 (0.995 → 1.000). RMS-specification crossings of ratio 1: 10 exported versus 8 band-passed.

## Group summaries and individual changes

Small shifts in group medians do not imply stability for every recording. The largest primary individual change is healthy9/iso90: 0.208 → 0.333 (+60.1%). RMS-specification crossings change from 10 to 8. These are descriptive sensitivity results, not clinical equivalence or evidence of practical clinical importance.

## RMS retention as a review indicator

RMS retention is band-passed RMS divided by mean-centered RMS. Its square is the ratio of filtered to centered mean square. The filter has transition attenuation and edge effects, so neither is a direct partition of spectral energy. Its median across recordings is 0.986. Records below 0.8: healthy9/iso90/uninv (0.62), healthy9/iso180/uninv (0.70), healthy8/iso90/uninv (0.71), healthy3/iso180/uninv (0.79). Low values indicate RMS attenuation under this specific filter, without identifying its physiological or acquisition cause. The 0.8 cut-off is a retrospective review heuristic; flagged records stay in every analysis.

## healthy8 / iso90 pair validity

The healthy8 iso90 uninv record lasts 2.00 s; the next shortest of all 88 records lasts 10.5 s. It meets the existing short-record rule (below 25% of the cohort-condition median length), and its RMS retention is 0.715: RMS decreases by 28.5% and mean square decreases by 48.9% under this filter. These are not percentages of energy proven to lie outside the band. A whole-record ratio between a ~25 s task and a ~2 s fragment does not compare like with like, so this pair is excluded from the primary presentation and retained as a sensitivity analysis. Its ratio is 25.2 exported and 64.6 band-passed. The rule and its promotion to an exclusion were decided retrospectively; the cause (truncated export, failed trial or acquisition issue) cannot be determined from the files.

Filter implementation: [SciPy butter](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.butter.html), [sosfiltfilt](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.sosfiltfilt.html). The band and review threshold are sensitivity choices, not validated clinical cut-offs.
